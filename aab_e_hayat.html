The Core Story

The Spiritual Sequel & Interest-Free Economic Journey

01

"Aab-e-Hayat" is a profound continuation of faith, love, responsibility, and transformation. It follows Salar Sikander and Imama Hashim as they step into a new chapter of life after the events of Peer-e-Kamil — a life shaped not only by love, but by marriage, family, faith, and responsibility.

At its heart are Salar and Imama — two people who have already travelled through extraordinary struggles, now learning that finding guidance is only the beginning. The real challenge lies in living by it when life becomes complicated, responsibilities grow, and every decision carries consequences.

ہر سفر منزل کی تلاش نہیں ہوتا، کچھ سفر انسان کو خود اُس کی پہچان تک لے جاتے ہیں۔

Their journey takes them beyond personal transformation and into a larger vision of an Islamic way of life. Salar becomes deeply involved in the idea of a Riba-free economic system Riba-free economic system , seeking to turn principles of faith into something that can exist within the realities of the modern world. "Every journey changes when the destination is no longer just about oneself."

The story explores the delicate balance between faith and worldly responsibilities, personal beliefs and practical life, family and ambition. It shows how spiritual growth does not end after finding Hidayah; instead, it demands patience, sacrifice, discipline, and the courage to live according to what one believes. Aab-e-Hayat is ultimately a story about what comes after transformation — when two people must build a life around the values they once struggled to discover. Because sometimes, finding the right path is only the beginning. The real journey is learning how to walk it.

The Mind Behind The Novel

About The Author

Umera Ahmed

Writer & Storyteller

Umera Ahmed is one of Pakistan’s most celebrated Urdu fiction writers and screenwriters. Known for her emotionally powerful and spiritually reflective storytelling, she often explores themes of faith, identity, morality, relationships, and human transformation.

Her writing has connected deeply with readers through stories that combine meaningful characters with questions about life, purpose, and spirituality. Aab-e-Hayat, the sequel to Peer-e-Kamil, continues this distinctive approach through the evolving lives of Salar Sikander and Imama Hashim.

Protagonists & Dynamics

Characters & Souls

01

Salar Sikander

Salar Sikander is one of the most complex and memorable characters in Aab-e-Hayat. He is exceptionally intelligent, gifted, and intellectually far ahead of those around him. Yet beneath that intelligence lies a deep sense of emptiness and confusion. Salar has the ability to understand difficult things, but he struggles to understand himself and the purpose of his existence. His intelligence initially becomes a source of arrogance and emotional distance. He questions almost everything and finds it difficult to accept answers simply because society or tradition provides them. His journey is therefore not simply about becoming a better person. It is about discovering meaning, confronting his own weaknesses, and moving from intellectual knowledge toward spiritual understanding. اس کے پاس سوال بہت تھے، مگر سکون کا جواب نہیں تھا۔ He had countless questions, but no answer that could give him peace. Salar's transformation is one of the central elements of the novel. His character demonstrates that intelligence can explain many things, but it cannot always provide purpose, peace, or guidance.

02

Imama Hashim

Imama Hashim represents faith, conviction, courage, and sacrifice. Her journey is fundamentally connected to her search for truth and her determination to remain loyal to her beliefs, even when doing so becomes extremely difficult. She is not a character whose strength comes from being loud or powerful. Her strength is found in her patience, restraint, faith, and ability to endure. Imama faces situations where choosing what she believes is right comes at a significant personal cost. She has to confront fear, uncertainty, family pressure, and loneliness while holding firmly to her convictions. وہ خاموش تھی، مگر اس کی خاموشی کمزوری نہیں تھی؛ وہ اپنے یقین کی آواز تھی۔ She was silent, but her silence was not weakness; it was the voice of her conviction. Imama's character shows that faith is not merely something a person claims to possess. True conviction is tested when holding on to it becomes difficult.
